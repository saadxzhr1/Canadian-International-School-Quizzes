Math & Physics Summary — Primary & Middle School

By Zahraoui Saad. Organized by subject and level — the formulas, diagrams, and memory hooks you actually need, not a textbook.

1. Number & Place Value Must know

Place value — read left to right: thousands, hundreds, tens, ones, then tenths, hundredths.

ThHTO
4582
TO.tenthshundredths
37.46
Comparing decimals: pad with zeros first — 6.8 = 6.80, so 6.80 > 6.08.
Rounding: look at the digit right after the target place. 0–4 → round down · 5–9 → round up.

2. Four Operations Must know

+ / − with decimals: line up the decimal points.

× with decimals: ignore the decimal points, multiply as whole numbers, then count total decimal places and place the point back.

2.4 × 1.55 → 24 × 155 = 3720 → 3 decimal places total
= 3.72

Division: know exact division, division with remainder, and decimal results.

95 ÷ 4 = 23 remainder 3 = 23.75
Order of operations: Brackets → Multiply/Divide → Add/Subtract

3. Fractions, Decimals & Percentages Must know

Fraction parts: numerator (top) / denominator (bottom) — e.g. in 3/4, 3 is the numerator, 4 the denominator.

3/4 = 0.75 = 75%
  • decimal → percentage: ×100
  • percentage → decimal: ÷100
  • fraction → decimal: numerator ÷ denominator
Percentage of a number: 15% of 400 = 0.15 × 400 = 60
Proportional method: 18 out of 20 → 18/20 × 100 = 90%

Fraction operations: simplify/equivalent fractions; add/subtract with a common denominator; multiply numerator × numerator and denominator × denominator; divide by multiplying by the reciprocal.

Numbers & Algebra

1. Signed Numbers Must know

+ × +− × −+ × −− × +
++−−
Multiplication/division: same signs → positive · different signs → negative.

Addition: same signs → add absolute values and keep the sign; different signs → subtract absolute values and keep the sign of the number with greater absolute value.

Subtraction: subtracting a number is adding its opposite.

a − b = a + (−b)

2. Fractions & Powers Must know

Add/subtract → common denominator. Multiply → numerator×numerator. Divide → multiply by the reciprocal.

2/3 ÷ 5/4 = 2/3 × 4/5

Powers: aⁿ = baseexponent

aᵐ × aⁿ = am+n

aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = am−n
a0 = 1   (a ≠ 0)

3. Algebra Must know

Vocabulary: variable, unknown, coefficient, term, like terms, expression, equation, solution.

Simplifying: 3x + 5x = 8x
Development: a(b+c) = ab+ac → 3(x+2) = 3x+6
Factorisation (reverse): 3x+6 = 3(x+2)
Inspector line: "Development removes parentheses and creates terms. Factorisation reverses this and creates a product."

4. Equations, Inequalities & Systems Must know

Equation: whatever you do to one side, do to the other.

3x+5=17 → 3x=12 → x=4

Inequality symbols: > < ≥ ≤

2x+3≤11 → 2x≤8 → x≤4
⚠️ Multiplying/dividing by a negative flips the inequality sign.

System of equations — two equations, same unknowns, solved by substitution or elimination.

x+y=10
x−y=2
The solution makes both equations true at the same time.

5. Proportionality & Functions Must know

Proportional: y = ax — straight line through the origin (0,0). y/x is constant.

Affine: y = ax+b — straight line, not necessarily through the origin. Crosses y-axis at (0,b).

Function: f(x)=2x+3 → f(4)=11 → 11 is the image of 4. Solve f(x)=11 → x=4 → 4 is the antecedent of 11.

slope a = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁)

a>0 → increasing · a<0 → decreasing · a=0 → constant

6. Square Roots Must know

√25 = 5
But x² = 25 → x = ±5 (since both 5² and (−5)² equal 25). √ itself only gives the positive root.

7. Algebraic Identities Must know

(a+b)² = a²+2ab+b²

(a−b)² = a²−2ab+b²

a²−b² = (a−b)(a+b)

Know both directions: development and recognizing them for factorisation.

Geometry

8. Triangles Must know

Classify triangles in two independent ways: by their sides and by their angles.

By sidesMeaning
Equilateral3 equal sides
Isosceles2 equal sides
Scalene3 different sides
By anglesMeaning
AcuteAll angles < 90°
Right-angledOne angle = 90°
ObtuseOne angle > 90°
Memory: a triangle can have both classifications — e.g. 40°, 40°, 100° = obtuse isosceles.

Parts: vertex = corner · side = edge · base = chosen reference side · height/altitude = perpendicular distance to the base · hypotenuse = side opposite the 90° angle.

A + B + C = 180°

9. Pythagoras & Thales Must know

a² + b² = c²

c = hypotenuse (side opposite the right angle). Use it when: two sides known → find the third.

AM/AB = AN/AC = MN/BC
Thales: parallel line inside a triangle → proportional segments.

10. Lines & Constructions Quick refresh

Median: from a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.

Median → midpoint

Altitude: from a vertex perpendicular to the opposite side (or its extension).

Altitude → 90°

Angle bisector: divides one angle into two equal angles.

Bisector → equal angles

Perpendicular bisector of [AB]: passes through the midpoint of AB and is perpendicular to AB.

Midpoint + 90°

Vocabulary: line · ray · segment · parallel · perpendicular · midpoint · vertex.

11. Central Symmetry Quick refresh

Equivalent to a 180° rotation around a center O. For A → A′ around O: OA = OA′, and O is the midpoint of [AA′].

12. Quadrilaterals Must know

Parallelogram: two pairs of opposite sides are parallel.

  • opposite sides are equal
  • opposite angles are equal
  • adjacent angles add to 180°
  • diagonals bisect each other

Rectangle: parallelogram + 4 right angles.

Its diagonals bisect each other and are equal.

Rhombus: parallelogram + 4 equal sides.

Its diagonals bisect each other at 90°.

Square: both a rectangle and a rhombus.

4 equal sides · 4 right angles · equal diagonals · perpendicular diagonals.

Hierarchy: Square ⊂ Rectangle ∩ Rhombus ⊂ Parallelogram.

13. Parallel Lines & Angles Must know

  • Corresponding (equal): ∠1=∠5, ∠2=∠6, ∠3=∠7, ∠4=∠8
  • Alternate interior (equal): ∠3=∠6, ∠4=∠5
  • Vertically opposite (equal): ∠1=∠4, ∠2=∠3, ∠5=∠8, ∠6=∠7
  • Co-interior / same-side (sum to 180°): ∠3+∠5=180°, ∠4+∠6=180°
  • Straight line: adjacent angles on a straight line sum to 180°. Around a point: angles sum to 360°.
One known angle determines all eight.

14. Circle Must know

Vocabulary: center, radius, diameter, chord, circumference. Diameter = a chord through the center.

d = 2r

C = 2πr

A = πr²

15. Coordinates & Triangle Inequality Quick refresh

Point A(x,y): x → horizontal, y → vertical. Origin = (0,0). The plane has four quadrants; read/plot points using x first, then y.

Horizontal distance: difference of x-values. Vertical distance: difference of y-values.

Symmetry about origin: (x,y) → (−x,−y)

Triangle inequality: the two shorter sides must sum to more than the longest.

a+b > c
|a−b| < c < a+b

16. Prisms & Cylinders Quick refresh

Prism: two identical, parallel polygonal bases.

V = Abase × h

Cylinder: two identical, parallel circular bases.

V = πr²h

Cuboid: V = LWH.   Cube: V = a³.

Statistics, Probability & Trigonometry

17. Statistics Must know

Vocabulary: population, individual, variable/character, value, effectif, frequency.

Effectif = count · Frequency = count ÷ total
mean = sum ÷ number of values

Median: sort the data, then take the middle value (or the mean of the two middle values when there is an even number of values).

Mode: most frequent value.   Range: maximum − minimum.

18. Probability Must know

P(E) = favorable equally-likely outcomes ÷ total equally-likely outcomes

— only valid when all outcomes are equally likely.

0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1   (0 = impossible, 1 = certain)

P(not E) = 1 − P(E)
Effectif = observed count · Frequency = observed proportion · Probability = theoretical chance

19. Trigonometry (3AC) Must know

sin θ = opp/hyp
cos θ = adj/hyp
tan θ = opp/adj
tan θ = sin θ / cos θ
SOH–CAH–TOA. Finds an unknown side (angle+side known) or an unknown angle (via sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹) — unlike Pythagoras, which needs no angle.

Exact special-triangle values: optional, only if extra time.

How to Handle the Interview

Don't prepare only to calculate. Be ready to do three things for any topic:

  1. Define — "An isosceles triangle has two equal sides."
  2. Solve — use the correct theorem/formula, get the result.
  3. Teach — explain why the method works, at a middle-schooler's level.
Teaching pattern: visual → concrete example → rule/formula → student exercise.

Priority Tiers

Tier 1 — Automatic

Arithmetic, fractions/decimals/percentages, measurement, perimeter/area/volume, basic geometry, signed numbers, algebra, equations, proportionality, triangles, quadrilaterals, Pythagoras, statistics, and basic probability.

Tier 2 — Quick refresh

1. Forces & Motion Must know

A force is a push or a pull acting on an object.

  • Pushing a door · Pulling a suitcase · Kicking a ball · A magnet attracting an object
A force can: start motion · stop motion · speed up · slow down · change direction · change shape.

Gravity — the force that pulls objects toward Earth.

Friction — a force that opposes motion when surfaces interact.

Useful frictionUnwanted friction
Walking · Braking · WritingWear of machine parts · Heat from rubbing

Air resistance / drag: a type of friction caused by air opposing motion — e.g. a parachute slowing a fall.

Simple motion: distance = how far · time = how long · speed = how quickly.

speed = distance ÷ time

2. States of Matter Must know

ShapeVolume

LiquidTakes container's shapeFixed
GasFills containerFills container
Vaporization = liquid → gas, in two ways: evaporation (at the surface, below boiling point) or boiling (throughout the liquid, at the boiling point).

3. Heat & Temperature Must know

Temperature: how hot or cold something is. Unit: °C. Measured with a thermometer.

Heat: thermal energy transferred from a hotter object to a colder one.

Heat always flows hot → cold. There is no separate "cold energy."

Heating/cooling causes changes of state:

Heating: ice → water → water vapor

Cooling: water vapor → water → ice

Conduction — direct contact, common in solids.
e.g. metal spoon heating up.

Convection — movement of liquid/gas.
e.g. warm air rising.

Radiation — no contact needed.
e.g. heat from the Sun or a fire.

4. Light Must know

Light source: produces its own light — Sun, candle, bulb, fire.

The Moon is not a light source — it reflects sunlight.

Light travels in straight lines through a uniform medium.

Transparent = clean glass · Translucent = frosted glass · Opaque = wood.

A shadow forms when an opaque object blocks light.

Reflection: light hits a surface and bounces back — mirrors, calm water, shiny metal.

We see objects because light reflects off them into our eyes.

5. Sound Must know

Sound is produced by vibrations — guitar string, speaker, vocal cords.

Sound needs a medium to travel through: solids, liquids, or gases (not a vacuum).

A source vibrates the surrounding medium → vibrations travel to our ears → detected and interpreted by the brain.

Sound can reflect off surfaces too, producing an echo — e.g. shouting near a cliff.

Loudness ↔ amplitude · Pitch ↔ frequency. No formulas needed at this level.

6. Electricity Must know

Closed circuit: complete path → current flows → bulb lights.

Open circuit: broken path → current stops → bulb off.

Cell = one electrical source. Battery = combination of cells.

Conductors (current flows easily)Insulators (resist current)
Copper · Aluminium · MetalsPlastic · Rubber · Glass · Dry wood
Wires: metal conductor inside + plastic insulation outside.

Current = the flow of electric charge through a circuit.

7. Magnets Must know

Magnetic field: the region around a magnet where magnetic effects act. Field lines run N → S outside the magnet.

Magnetic materials: iron, steel are strongly attracted. Plastic is not.

Compass: uses magnetism to show direction.

Everyday uses: refrigerator doors · magnetic catches · speakers · compasses.

How to Handle the Physics Interview

At primary level, the interview is more likely to probe explanation and demonstration than calculation.

  1. Define — plain-language definition a child would understand.
  2. Everyday example — connect it to something the student already experiences.
  3. Simple demo idea — how you'd show it hands-on (e.g. torch + objects for opaque/translucent/transparent).
Teaching pattern: everyday example → simple demo → vocabulary → student prediction/observation.

Fast demo ideas per lesson

  • Forces: push/pull a toy car, compare rolling on carpet vs smooth floor (friction)
  • States of matter: ice cube melting in hand vs on a plate
  • Heat: metal spoon vs wooden spoon in warm water (conduction)
  • Light: torch through glass / frosted plastic / cardboard
  • Sound: ruler twanged off a desk edge — pitch changes with length
  • Electricity: simple bulb-battery-switch circuit, open vs closed
  • Magnets: sorting a mixed pile of objects — attracted vs not
1AC — First Year

1. Matter & Environment Must know

States of water: solid, liquid, gas — properties of each, plus the water cycle.

Measuring volume & mass

V = L × W × H

1 L = 1 dm³

1 mL = 1 cm³

Density

ρ = m/V

Units: g/cm³ or kg/m³ · 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³

Pressure

P = F/A

1 Pa = 1 N/m² · 1 hPa = 100 Pa

Same force + smaller area → greater pressure.

Atmospheric pressure: air exerts pressure, measured with a barometer. Concept level only.

Particle model (builds on primary states of matter):

Solidparticles close, vibrate around fixed positions
Liquidparticles close, move past each other
Gasparticles far apart, move freely
Heating generally increases average kinetic energy of particles.

Heat, temperature & changes of state: temperature = how hot/cold · heat = thermal energy transferred hotter → colder.

Temperature stays constant during a change of state — the plateau on a heating/cooling graph. Energy goes into changing state, not raising temperature.
Vaporization = liquid → gas, two ways: slowly at the surface (evaporation) or throughout the liquid at boiling point (boiling).

Mixtures & solutions

Pure substance · homogeneous mixture · heterogeneous mixture · dissolution · solute · solvent · solution.

Solution = homogeneous mixture. e.g. salt + water → salt solution.

Separation of mixtures

Filtrationinsoluble solid + liquid
Decantationseparate phases/layers
Evaporationrecover dissolved solid
Distillationdifferent boiling points, then condensation

2. Electricity Must know

Simple circuit parts: generator/cell/battery · wires · switch · receiver.

Closed circuit → current flows. Open circuit → current stops.

Effects of current: thermal (heating) · luminous (light) · chemical · magnetic.

ConductorsInsulators
Copper · Aluminium · MetalsPlastic · Rubber · Glass

I current → ampere (A), ammeter in series. V voltage → volt (V), voltmeter in parallel.

I = I₁ = I₂

V = V₁ + V₂
I = I₁ + I₂

Resistance (R): unit Ω. Basic idea — resistance opposes current.

Electrical safety: insulation · short circuits · excessive current · electric shock · circuit breakers/fuses · safe handling.

2AC — Second Year

3. Air, Atmosphere & Chemistry Must know

Air is a mixture of gases — mainly nitrogen and oxygen. Atmosphere, air movement, pollution.

Atom = basic particle of an element. Molecule = group of atoms bonded together.

Chemical reactions produce new substances.

Physical changeChemical change
ice → water (same substance)new substance(s) formed

Combustion: role of oxygen — e.g. carbon combustion, butane combustion. Important

Conservation of mass (Lavoisier): total mass of reactants = total mass of products. Simple reaction interpretation only, no stoichiometry yet.

Natural vs synthetic substances: know the distinction with everyday examples.

4. Light & Optics Must know

Sources & receivers: Sun = source. Moon = receiver/reflects sunlight.

Light and colours: light can be dispersed into colours.

Light travels in a straight line in a uniform medium. Speed of light ≈ 300,000 km/s.

Applications of rectilinear propagation: shadows · eclipses · pinhole camera (camera obscura).

Media types: transparent · translucent · opaque (recap from primary).

Optical instruments quick review: magnifying glass, eye, simple instruments — basic purpose only.

5. Electricity — AC & Domestic Must know

DC: current supplied by a battery/cell (constant direction).

AC: current changes direction periodically (sinusoidal).

Oscilloscope: reads period T, frequency f = 1/T, and amplitude Umax off the trace — distinguishes continuous from alternating voltage.

Domestic electrical installation:

Phase/live · Neutral · Earth/ground — plus basic electrical safety.

3AC — Third Year (highest exam weight)

6. Materials & Chemistry

Atoms and ions must know

Atom · nucleus/electrons (basic level) · electrically neutral atom · ion · positive/negative ion.

Oxidation of metals important — e.g. iron, aluminium react with oxygen in air.

Combustion of organic materials — oxygen, products, safety/environmental implications.

Acids and bases must know

Measuring pH, interpreting the scale. Quick review: reactions of metals with acids/bases (gas production), ion identification tests, materials/health/environment hazards.

7. Mechanics Must know

Motion depends on the reference frame. Trajectory: rectilinear · circular · curvilinear.

v = d/t

vavg = total distance ÷ total time

A force is a push or pull. Effects: change motion, speed, direction, or shape.

Characteristics of a force: point of application · direction · sense · magnitude — represented by an arrow.

Equilibrium of two forces: same magnitude · opposite sense · same line of action → Fnet = 0

Mass: kg, amount of matter — stays the same when location changes.

W = mg

Weight (N): a vertical, downward force applied at the center of gravity — it depends on the gravitational field.
